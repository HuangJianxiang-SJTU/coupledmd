GPCR-G MD · resource plan Target: NAR Web Server Issue · Planning draft

Research & development plan

The coupled state, in motion.

A web resource serving molecular dynamics of active-state GPCR–G-protein ternary complexes, organized by G-protein family, with a precomputed coupling-resolved analysis layer. It is the gap between the receptor dynamics in GPCRmd and the static complex models in GproteinDB.

01   Positioning

The niche, stated plainly

Two resources already sit next to this idea. GPCRmd serves molecular dynamics of GPCRs, organized around the receptor. GproteinDB serves GPCR–G-protein complexes, but as static AlphaFold2-Multimer models together with coupling data, with no dynamics. A 2025 Nature Communications study from the GPCRmd group has already reported allosteric sites and lateral gateways mined from large-scale GPCR MD. The space that none of them occupies is the dynamics of the coupled ternary complex, resolved by G-protein family, with the same receptor compared across its different G-protein partners.

That intersection is what the 222-system database is. The resource is therefore not a second GPCRmd and not a dynamic GproteinDB. It is the coupled state in motion, and its single most distinctive offering is the within-receptor partner-switching view that no static or receptor-only resource can provide. The plan below is built to occupy that intersection cleanly and to interoperate with GPCRdb and GproteinDB by cross-linking rather than reproducing their data.

Read before committing

The 2025 Nature Communications paper (allosteric sites and lateral gateways from large-scale GPCR MD) overlaps your Paper 1 more than it overlaps this server. Read it first and reposition Paper 1 around the ternary, family-resolved, partner-switching angle. The server survives that overlap because its value is the coupled-state data and the G-protein-resolved organization, neither of which that study provides.

02   Competitive landscape

Three adjacent resources, and the empty cell

GPCRmd

Receptor MD · viewer + toolbox

Community platform serving GPCR molecular dynamics with a web viewer and analysis tools. Receptor-centric, broad receptor coverage, mostly apo, inactive, or active receptor systems. Updated through 2025.

GproteinDB

Static complexes · coupling data

GPCR–G-protein complexes as AlphaFold2-Multimer models plus refined experimental structures, integrated coupling datasets, and interface analysis tools. Structures and couplings, not trajectories.

GPCR-G MD (this plan)

Ternary complex dynamics · family-resolved

Molecular dynamics of agonist-bound receptor–G-protein ternary complexes, organized by Gα family, with a precomputed pocket, gateway, and engagement-geometry layer and a same-receptor partner-switching view.

The 2025 Nature Communications analysis (allosteric sites and lateral gateways) is an analysis built on GPCRmd-style data; it is prior art for the analysis layer, not a competing resource. The plan cites it as such and differentiates the server on data scope and organization.

03   Resource scope

What the resource contains

The core is 222 molecular dynamics systems covering 182 unique receptors across the four G-protein families, each simulated as an agonist-bound ternary complex, for roughly 332 microseconds of aggregate sampling. Each system carries its trajectory, a matched topology, retained velocities and forces where available, the explicit bilayer, the bound ligand and ions, and a metadata record mapped to GPCRdb generic numbering.

The honest composition of the database

The database is not perfectly uniform, and the metadata must say so, but the one axis that is uniform is the force field. All systems use CHARMM36, verified from the chamber-format CHARMM topologies of the membrane-embedded systems and the CHARMM-GUI origin of the protein-only systems; the earlier “AMBER” label referred to the simulation engine, pmemd, not the force field. The real heterogeneity is trajectory type. Most systems, 212 of 222, were run as full complexes in an explicit POPC bilayer with AMBER pmemd, while ten were run as protein-only GROMACS trajectories with no bilayer, which makes the latter usable for pocket and engagement analysis but not for the lipid-facing gateway analysis. Every system derives from an experimentally determined cryo-EM or X-ray ternary structure, and the set contains no homology or AlphaFold-built receptors. Recording the force field, the trajectory type, the bilayer status, and the structural provenance per system is what lets the resource claim a uniform force field and a uniform lipid environment for the lipid-facing analyses without overstating it.

The precomputed analysis layer

This is the layer that separates the resource from a trajectory dump. It carries, per system and per family, the allosteric pocket occupancy maps and the consensus pocket atlas, the lateral gateway penetration metrics, the α5 engagement geometry, the druggable pocket nominations, and the within-receptor reorganization across G-protein partners. These are the products of the companion study and become the showcase use cases on the server.

05   User-facing functionality

Server features

Browse, search, and filter

Entry by receptor, GPCR class, Gα family, ligand type, and functional coupling, with the four-family color system used consistently so a user reads partner identity at a glance. Cross-links resolve each receptor to its GPCRdb and GproteinDB entries.

Per-system pages

Each system gets a page with its full metadata record, an embedded trajectory viewer, and the precomputed plots for that system: backbone deviation and per-residue fluctuation, pocket occupancy, gateway penetration, and α5 geometry. Structural provenance, force field, trajectory type, and bilayer status are shown without burial.

Trajectory viewer

An in-browser viewer streams the trajectory rather than forcing a download, using an established streaming layer for molecular dynamics on the web paired with a WebGL structure viewer. The visualization stream is decimated so that even large systems load quickly.

The atlas browsers

Two dedicated browsers expose the consensus pocket atlas and the lateral gateway atlas, each filterable by family and linked back to the contributing systems. The druggable pocket nominations are presented as a ranked, exportable table with the druggability proxy and the family selectivity status shown honestly.

The within-receptor comparison tool

The signature feature. A user selects a promiscuous receptor, picks two G-protein partners, and sees the pocket set and gateway profile side by side with the reorganization quantified. This view exists nowhere else because it requires dynamics of multiple ternary complexes of the same receptor.

Download and programmatic access

A download center serves trajectories, topologies, and the analysis artifacts as small structured files, and a documented REST interface exposes the metadata and the precomputed analyses for scripted use. Full trajectories with velocities and forces resolve to a permanent archive by DOI.

06   Implementation

Architecture and the storage problem

The shape is conventional: a metadata database, an object store for trajectories and artifacts, an API backend, and a JavaScript frontend with an embedded streaming viewer. The one genuinely hard engineering constraint is storage. All-atom sampling of 332 microseconds, with velocities and forces and an explicit bilayer, runs to multiple terabytes. Serving that directly for visualization is neither necessary nor wise, so the data is tiered.

TierContentsServed asRough scale
Visualizationsingle replica, heavily strided coordinates, no waterstreamed to the in-browser viewertens of MB per system
Analysisprecomputed pockets, gateways, geometry, occupancy gridssmall structured files and the APIKB to a few MB per system
Full dataall replicas, full frame rate, velocities and forcespermanent archive, fetched by DOI on requestmulti-TB in aggregate
 The live server holds the first two tiers. The third lives in a permanent repository, which also solves longevity: the data outlives the server.

Deployment is containerized for reproducibility and to make mirroring and handoff straightforward. The live service needs a persistent host with adequate bandwidth, which is a different machine from the GPU workstation used to produce the data. Static assets and the visualization tier sit behind a cache so the viewer stays responsive under load.

07   Data stewardship

Standardization, FAIR, and deposition

The mixed origin of the trajectories is a stewardship task, not a flaw to hide. Downloadable formats are homogenized to widely readable topology and trajectory pairs, the trajectory type and format are documented in the metadata against the single shared CHARMM36 force field, and every file carries a checksum. The full dataset is deposited in a permanent archive with assigned DOIs under an open license, so the data is citable and persistent independent of the live server. An MD-specific repository or a general scientific archive both work; the choice is made on which gives the cleanest DOI-per-system granularity. Metadata follows community molecular dynamics conventions so the records are interoperable, and releases are versioned so that a citation resolves to a fixed snapshot.

08   Showcase

Featured analyses, drawn from the companion study

The resource paper and the atlas paper reinforce each other. The atlas paper is the discovery; the server is the resource that makes the discovery explorable and reusable. Four analyses become the showcase use cases, each linked from the front page to the systems behind it: the consensus allosteric pocket atlas with its recovery of known sites, the depth-gated lateral gateway atlas, the druggable pocket nominations validated against held-out modulator sites, and the within-receptor reorganization across partners. Each use case cites the atlas paper, and the atlas paper cites the server as the home of its data. This cross-citation is the cleanest way to satisfy a reviewer that the resource is real and used.

09   Longevity

Sustainability and maintenance

A journal that publishes web servers expects them to stay up for years, so longevity is designed in rather than promised. Three mechanisms carry it. The full data lives in a permanent archive by DOI, so the scientific record survives even a lapse in the live service. The container images and the ingest scripts are public, so the server can be rebuilt or mirrored by anyone, including an institutional host after the student graduates. And the update model follows the community-contribution pattern, where new ternary-complex simulations can be ingested through the same pipeline, giving the resource a path to grow rather than ossify. A named institutional handoff and at least one mirror should be arranged before submission, because a reviewer will ask who keeps it alive.

10   Execution

Development roadmap

Durations are relative and assume part-time work alongside the dissertation, with the analysis layer already largely produced. Bars show approximate effort, not calendar precision.

P0
Data audit and standardization

Freeze the cohort and document it

Finalize the inventory, homogenize downloadable formats, record structural provenance, force field, trajectory type, and bilayer status per system, and compute checksums. This is the step that makes every later claim about the dataset exact, including the resolved fact that the force field is uniformly CHARMM36.

P1
Metadata model and ingest

Schema, database, and loader

Define the metadata schema with GPCRdb generic numbering and coupling annotations, build the database, and write the ingest that loads every system reproducibly.

P2
Analysis layer to disk

Serialize the precomputed products

Write the pocket, gateway, geometry, and reorganization products to stable file and JSON schemas keyed to systems. Most of this exists from the companion study and needs packaging, not recomputation.

P3
Backend, tiering, deposit

API, storage tiers, and the permanent archive

Stand up the API, build the three storage tiers, decimate the visualization tier, and deposit the full data to the archive with DOIs. The deposit can proceed in parallel with frontend work.

P4
Frontend core

Browse, search, per-system pages, viewer

Build the search and filter surface, the per-system pages, and the embedded streaming trajectory viewer. This is the largest single block of frontend effort.

P5
Differentiating tools

Atlas browsers and the partner-switching view

Build the pocket and gateway atlas browsers and the within-receptor comparison tool. The comparison tool is the signature feature and deserves the most polish.

P6
Documentation

Tutorials and worked examples

Write the documentation, a getting-started tour, and a few worked analysis examples that double as the figures and use cases in the manuscript.

P7
Deploy and harden

Stress test and closed beta

Deploy to the persistent host, load test the viewer and download paths, arrange the mirror and the institutional handoff, and run a closed beta with collaborators to surface gaps.

P8
Manuscript

Write and submit to NAR

Write the resource paper around the server and its use cases, cross-cite the atlas paper, complete the requirements checklist below, and submit to the Web Server Issue.

11   Publication

NAR submission strategy

The resource fits the annual Web Server Issue, whose submission window falls in the first part of the year, with a Database Issue as the alternative if the emphasis shifts toward the data rather than the tools. Confirm the current call and deadline on the journal site before planning around it. The decision between the two issues turns on framing: lead with the interactive tools and the partner-switching view for the Web Server Issue, lead with the curated coupled-state dataset for the Database Issue.

What a reviewer will check, and how the plan answers it

  • Is it freely available without login, and is it up. Open access by design, containerized, mirrored, and monitored before submission.
  • Does it overlap existing servers. The differentiation matrix is the answer: ternary dynamics, family organization, and the partner-switching view are the cells no neighbor fills, and the server cross-links rather than duplicates GPCRdb and GproteinDB.
  • Does it actually work. The viewer loads, search returns, the API responds, and downloads resolve, all verified in the closed beta.
  • Is it useful and documented. The four showcase use cases, drawn from the companion study, demonstrate use, and the documentation gives a path in for a non-specialist.
  • Will it persist. The permanent DOI archive and the institutional handoff answer the longevity question directly.

Sequencing the two papers

Submit or post the atlas paper first or in parallel, so the server paper can cite it as the companion analysis. The atlas demonstrates the resource's scientific value, and the server gives the atlas a permanent, explorable home. Each makes the other stronger to a reviewer.

13   Identity

Naming candidates

A name should signal the coupled-state, G-protein focus and stay clear of GproteinDB to avoid confusion. A few options, with a recommendation.

CoupledMDRecommended. Names the distinctive thing, the coupled ternary state in motion, and is not easily confused with neighbors.
GPCR-G MDDescriptive and self-explanatory; echoes GPCRmd, which is both an advantage and a risk of being read as derivative.
TernaryMDEmphasizes the ternary complex; clear but less specific to GPCRs.
GproteinMDParallels GproteinDB and signals dynamics, but the closeness to that name may confuse users.
